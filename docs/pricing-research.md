**Takeaways**
- Going rate for a small landlord is **KSh 75–100 per unit**; mid-size portfolios pay **~40–60**; large agency books fall to **~15–50**.
- Most rivals use flat tiers, so a landlord near the bottom of a tier overpays. Per-occupied-unit pricing (masterplan §4.9) is fairer and easy to explain.
- Several charge for vacant units or sit rent in their own accounts; Rentife does neither.

## Rentife prices (per occupied unit, per month, excl. VAT)

| Plan | Units | Per unit | Minimum | 12 units | 60 units | 300 units |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Starter | 1–30 | KSh 80 | KSh 800 | KSh 960 | — | — |
| Growth | 31–100 | KSh 60 | KSh 2,400 | — | KSh 3,600 | — |
| Agency | 101+ or any agent | KSh 40 | KSh 6,000 | — | — | KSh 12,000 |

**Why these numbers**
- **Starter KSh 80** sits under the KSh 100 market rate while keeping premium positioning; the masterplan's 12-unit Kasarani landlord pays **KSh 960** vs 1,500–3,000 at most rivals.
- **Growth KSh 60** matches mid-market (Bomahut, EazzyRent) while including matching, receipts, reminders and the tax centre that rivals reserve for higher tiers.
- **Agency KSh 40** with a KSh 6,000 floor is in line with MilikiHQ Business and Nyumba Zetu's volume rates, and includes owner statements, commission and owner portals.
- **No price cliffs:** each minimum equals the top of the plan below (30 × 80 = 2,400; 100 × 60 = 6,000), so a bill never drops or jumps when a portfolio crosses a boundary.
- **Margin check:** reminders and receipts cost roughly KSh 5–10 per unit per month in SMS/WhatsApp fees, leaving healthy margin even at KSh 40.
